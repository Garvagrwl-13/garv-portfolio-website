An apology for memorisation.

Rote learning has a bad name. We treat it as the opposite of understanding — the thing teachers do when they've run out of better ideas. But Sanskrit pedagogy, for two and a half millennia, has treated it as something closer to prayer.

The student who has the Aṣṭādhyāyī by heart does not know less than the one who can look it up. She knows it differently. The whole grammar sits in her mouth, available the way a melody is available — not as data to be retrieved but as shape already in motion.

What I want to argue in this essay is small: that there is a kind of attention which only memorisation produces, and that we have, somewhat unwisely, decided we can live without it.
