Garv Aggarwal · The Library · 05 — Protect
a manifesto · in seven theses

Protect.

What this is Not a project. A position. The reason any of the other work exists at all — written down so I can hold myself to it, and so a stranger reading this site knows exactly what's at stake.

Sanskrit is not dying. It is being forgotten — which is worse, because forgetting is a thing we choose.

It survives only as long as someone — a child in a paṭhaśālā, a researcher with an editor open, a translator at three in the morning — decides it is worth the trouble.

This room is a list of those troubles, and the people taking them.

Garv Aggarwal · 2025

Seven theses.

The whole argument, compressed. Each is unpacked in a longer essay below.

i.

Sanskrit is not a religion

Confusing it with one is the single biggest reason it gets refused, and the second biggest reason it gets misused.

ii.

It is also not dead

A language with active speakers, active poets, and a working morphology is alive — even if we wish, for our own reasons, that it weren't.

iii.

Memorisation is a technology

Of preservation, of attention, of community. Replacing it with search is not progress; it's a trade we should make with both eyes open.

iv.

Translation is essential, and dangerous

Necessary for circulation, fatal if mistaken for the thing itself. Translate often, never quietly.

v.

The script is not the language

Sanskrit existed for centuries in many scripts and in none. Devanāgarī is a beautiful guest, not the landlord.

vi.

Open the door wider

Free texts, free dictionaries, free pedagogy. Anything else is a gate built by people who confused tradition with property.

vii.

Build with it, not around it

Models, tools, education platforms — let the language sit at the centre of the design, not the bottom of a footnote.
